The purpose of this survey is to identify the main factors contributing to employee turnover in entry-level and lower-level positions at the company. Describe your target population, sampling frame, sampling units, and observational units:
```
- Target Population: All currently employed full-time and part-time employees who have been with the company for at least three months, across all departments and locations, with a particular focus on entry-level and lower-level positions.
- Sampling Frame: The most recent HR employee roster, which includes names, job titles, departments, employment status (full-time/part-time), tenure, and company email addresses of all eligible employees.
- Sampling Units: Individual employees on the HR roster who meet the eligibility criteria.
- Observational Units: The same individual employees who complete the questionnaire and provide survey responses.
- Overall Sampling Strategy: A stratified random sampling strategy will be used, with strata based on department and job level (entry-level, lower-level, and senior positions). Within each stratum, employees will be randomly selected and invited to participate. Entry-level and lower-level positions may be oversampled to ensure sufficient data from the primary target group. This approach helps ensure the sample is representative across departments while focusing on the positions with high turnover rates.
```

## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

```
1. Sample type
- A probability-based sample survey with a cross-sectional design using a two-phase sampling approach. First, households are selected using stratified sampling from the survey frame; second, one individual aged 15 or older is randomly selected from each sampled household
2. Sample size
- A field sample of approximately 50,000 units was selected. About 40,000 invitation letters were sent, with an expected completion of approximately 24,000 questionnaires.
3. Target population
- All persons 15 years of age and older living in the ten provinces of Canada. Full-time residents of institutions (residing for more than six months) are excluded
4. Sampling frame
- A combined sampling frame integrating landline and cellular telephone numbers derived from administrative sources and linked to Statistics Canada's dwelling frame
5. Survey mode(s) 
- Mixed-mode data collection:
- Computer Assisted Telephone Interviewing (CATI)
- Electronic Questionnaire (EQ) for online self-completion Respondents could choose between English and French. Proxy responses were not permitted. Average completion time was 44 minutes.
6. Timeline
- Reference period: Past 12 months preceding interview date
- Collection period: September 4, 2018 to December 28, 2018
- Data release: January 26, 2021
- Survey frequency: Every 5 years
7. Response rate. The overall response rate was 41.9%
8. Weights
- WGHT_PER: The principal weighting factor for person-level estimates.
- Calibration: Weights are adjusted to match known population counts (by age, sex, and province) and income distributions from the 2017 Canadian Income Survey.
- Bootstrap weights: Provided for design-based variance estimation.
9. Data processing. Data processing used the Social Survey Processing Environment (SSPE). Key steps included:
- Data capture and coding.
- Automated and manual edits at macro and micro levels.
- CATI system with built-in valid range checks and automatic flow control.
- Head office review of interviewer comments
10. Cleaning, imputation, etc
- Edits: Family relationship checks, consistency edits (e.g., age vs. birth date), and flow edits.
- Imputation: Primarily "nearest-neighbor" donor imputation using a score function; mean imputation used when donor imputation was not feasible.
- Steps: Imputation carried out in nine steps covering: personal/family income, formal volunteering, informal volunteering, and donation variables.
- Income data: Personal income obtained from 2017 T1FF tax records for 81.9% of respondents; family income obtained for 81.7% of households via direct linkage
11. Sources of error
- Sampling error: Estimates based on a sample vary from sample to sample; bootstrap method used to estimate sampling variability.
- Non-sampling errors: a Coverage error: Households without telephones are excluded. b Non-response error: Occurred at both household and individual levels. c Response error and processing error.
12. Limitations, known biases, etc
- Coverage bias: Households without telephone service are excluded, though this is a small portion of the population.
- Geographic exclusion: Residents of the three territories (Yukon, Northwest Territories, Nunavut) are excluded.
- Institutional exclusion: Full-time institutional residents are not included.
- Non-response bias: With a 41.9% response rate, potential bias is addressed through weight adjustments using administrative data on non-responding households
13. Link to documentation and any additional sources used
- Statistics Canada. (2021). General Social Survey: Giving, Volunteering and Participating (GSS), Cycle 33, 2018 (Record no. 4430).Retrieved from: https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&Id=796234 Record number: 4430

```
